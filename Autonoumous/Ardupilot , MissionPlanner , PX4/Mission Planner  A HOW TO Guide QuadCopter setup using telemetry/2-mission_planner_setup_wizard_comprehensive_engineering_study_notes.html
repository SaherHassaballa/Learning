🎯 What This Video Is About

This tutorial demonstrates the rapid initial commissioning of an autonomous quadcopter running ArduCopter on a Pixhawk-class flight controller using the integrated Mission Planner Setup Wizard.

Operating over a pre-established wireless telemetry link, the instructor bypasses disjointed individual hardware menus and executes a streamlined, 16-step calibration pipeline. The video systematically walks through multirotor airframe geometry definition, 6-axis accelerometer reference frame sampling, 3D live magnetometer (compass) offset mapping, battery telemetry scaling, radio control (RC) pulse endpoint boundary registration ($1000\,\mu\text{s} - 2000\,\mu\text{s}$), baseline flight mode assignment (`Stabilize` and `RTL`), and pre-arm safety interlock evaluations.

🧠 Core Concepts

1. The Automated Configuration Pipeline (Mission Planner Wizard)
What is it? A guided linear software wizard embedded in Mission Planner (`Initial Setup -> Wizard`) that aggregates mandatory hardware configurations and sensor calibrations into an unbroken sequence.
Why do we use it? Prior to initial flight, an uncalibrated autopilot has undefined mathematical offsets for gravity, magnetic North, and stick travel. The wizard ensures no foundational safety calibration is omitted before writing parameters to the flight controller's onboard non-volatile memory (EEPROM/FRAM).
How does it work? The wizard queries the flight controller over MAVLink, prompts the operator through physical vehicle manipulations, verifies sensor tolerance boundaries, calculates offset and scaling matrices, and commits the resulting calibration parameters sequentially.
Where is it used? During bench commissioning of newly built or freshly flashed multirotors, planes, and rovers running ArduPilot.
Example: Rather than manually hunting for `FRAME_CLASS`, `INS_ACCOFFS`, `COMPASS_OFS`, and `RC3_MIN` inside the Full Parameter List, the wizard prompts you to place the drone in 6 positions, spin it in a sphere, and sweep your sticks, writing those parameters automatically.
Prerequisites: Established MAVLink connection (via USB or wireless SiK telemetry radios) and flashed ArduCopter firmware.
2. 6-Axis Orthogonal Gravity Vector Calibration
What is it? A mathematical sampling procedure where the flight controller measures the static acceleration vector of Earth's gravity ($1g \approx 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2$) across all six orthogonal spatial orientations.
Why do we use it? MEMS accelerometers suffer from manufacturing zero-bias errors and sensitivity variations along their physical $X$, $Y$, and $Z$ axes. Without calibration, the Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) cannot accurately compute horizontal level or distinguish dynamic aircraft acceleration from gravitational pull.
How does it work? The vehicle is placed in six orthogonal positions: Level, Left side, Right side, Nose Down, Nose Up, and Inverted. In each position, gravity acts purely along one primary axis ($\pm 1g$). ArduPilot solves a linear system of equations to extract scale factors and zero-biases ($X_{\text{offset}}, Y_{\text{offset}}, Z_{\text{offset}}$).
Where is it used? In the Inertial Measurement Unit (IMU) attitude estimation pipeline of all aerial robotics.
Example: When resting level, the $Z$-axis accelerometer reads $+9.81\,\text{m/s}^2$ while $X$ and $Y$ read $0.0\,\text{m/s}^2$. When placed nose down, the $X$-axis registers $-9.81\,\text{m/s}^2$.
Prerequisites: A stable, vibration-free workbench and rigid flight controller mounting.
3. Live 3D Spherical Magnetometer Calibration
What is it? A live sampling process where the drone is tumbled through all three rotational spatial axes ($360^\circ$ Roll, Pitch, and Yaw) while the software measures surrounding magnetic field vectors.
Why do we use it? Earth's weak geomagnetic field ($0.25 - 0.65\,\text{Gauss}$) is distorted by structural drone components: ferromagnetic screws/spacers (**Hard-Iron distortion**) and induced magnetic fields from conductive materials (**Soft-Iron distortion**). Calibration cancels these distortions.
How does it work? As the vehicle is tumbled in the air, the compass collects 3D magnetic coordinates $[B_x, B_y, B_z]$. In a clean environment, these points form a perfect sphere centered at $(0,0,0)$. Hard-iron offsets shift the sphere's origin; soft-iron effects distort it into an ellipsoid. ArduPilot fits the collected points to a sphere and computes corrective transformation matrices.
Where is it used? In primary directional heading estimation to prevent GPS "toilet-bowling" during autonomous position-hold modes (`Loiter`, `RTL`, `Auto`).
Prerequisites: Wireless telemetry connection (to avoid cable tangling) and freedom from nearby ferrous metals.
4. Normalized PWM Radio Control Endpoint Mapping
What is it? The procedure of sweeping all transmitter control sticks, switches, and potentiometers to register their physical travel limits as microsecond pulse widths.
Why do we use it? Different RC transmitters (FrSky, RadioMaster, Spektrum, Futaba) output non-identical pulse ranges (e.g., one transmitter outputs $988 - 2012\,\mu\text{s}$, while another outputs $1004 - 1996\,\mu\text{s}$). ArduPilot must establish the true low endpoint, neutral center, and high endpoint for every channel.
How does it work? The operator moves gimbals into all four diagonal corners and sweeps all knobs/switches. Mission Planner tracks the peak minimum and maximum microsecond values, setting: $$ \text{Min} \approx 1000\,\mu\text{s}, \quad \text{Trim} \approx 1500\,\mu\text{s}, \quad \text{Max} \approx 2000\,\mu\text{s} $$
Where is it used? All multirotor and fixed-wing receiver input channels (`RC1_MIN` through `RC8_MAX`).
Prerequisites: Bound RC transmitter and receiver, powered and transmitting to Pixhawk `RC IN`.

Absolute Safety Rule: Complete Propeller Removal!
Propellers must be 100% removed from all motor shafts before executing the Setup Wizard. The wizard includes an RC calibration sequence and an arming test. 🛠️ Practical Implementation: Step-by-Step Wizard Walkthrough

Follow this sequential procedure demonstrated by The Drone Dojo to commission an autonomous drone in approximately 10 minutes:

  1. Prerequisites & Workspace Preparation: Ensure the drone is connected to Mission Planner via wireless telemetry (solid green LEDs confirmed on both SiK radio modules, baud rate set to 57600). Verify all propellers are completely removed from the motors.
  2. Launch the Setup Wizard: Navigate to the top ribbon menu and click INITIAL SETUP. In the left-hand navigation column, click Wizard. A multi-step window will launch.
  3. Frame Selection (Step 1): Select the airframe format: choose `Copter`, then select `X` Configuration. Click Next.
    💡
    Why the X Frame? An "X" frame configuration places the four arms at $45^\circ$ offsets from the longitudinal axis, leaving a wide, clear forward field-of-view (FOV) for cameras and sensors between motors 1 and 2.
  4. 6-Axis Accelerometer Calibration (Step 2): Click Start Now. Sequentially place the drone in the six required physical orientations, ensuring the airframe is completely stationary before clicking Continue for each:
    • Level: Rest the drone flat on its landing gear on a level surface. Click Continue.
    • Left Side: Tip the drone $90^\circ$ onto its left arms/landing skids. Click Continue.
    • Right Side: Tip the drone $90^\circ$ onto its right arms/landing skids. Click Continue.
    • Nose Down: Orient the front arms/camera pointing vertically down toward the floor. Click Continue.
    • Nose Up: Orient the front arms/camera pointing vertically up toward the ceiling. Click Continue.
    • Back (Inverted): Flip the drone completely upside down.
      ⚠️ Workshop Tip: The elevated GPS mast on the back of the drone prevents it from resting flat. Hold the airframe securely by hand upside down, visually verify it is approximately level, keep it still, and click Continue.
  5. Live Compass Calibration (Step 3): Click Live Calibration. Pick up the drone in both hands. Smoothly tumble, pitch, roll, and yaw the drone through all 3D rotational axes in the air.
    Watch the sampling points populate the graphical sphere in Mission Planner. Continue rotating until the progress bar reaches 100% and a dialog appears stating: "New compass offsets have been set." Click OK.
    🟢
    Telemetry vs. USB Cable: The instructor specifically highlights that performing live compass calibration over a wireless telemetry link is significantly easier and safer than using a USB cable, which tangles, restricts 3D rotation, and risks breaking the flight controller's USB connector.
  6. Battery Monitor Setup (Step 4): Configure the battery telemetry module:
    • Monitor: Select Analog Voltage and Current (or 4: Voltage and Current).
    • Sensor: Select 9: Other / PX4 (matches standard Pixhawk Power Modules).
    • APM / Flight Controller Version: Select Pixhawk / PX4.
    • Battery Capacity: Enter your battery pack's nominal milliamp-hour rating (e.g., enter 3000 for a $3000\,\text{mAh}$ pack). Click Next.
  7. Sonar / Rangefinder (Step 5): If no downward-facing sonar or LiDAR sensor is installed, ensure the dropdown is set to `None` and click Next.
  8. Radio Control Calibration (Step 6): Power on your RC transmitter. (Ensure all top toggle switches are resting in the **UP / AWAY** position, or the transmitter will block boot-up with a switch warning). Click Calibrate Radio, then click OK on the propeller safety reminder:
    • Left Gimbal Sweep (Channels 3 & 4): Push the left stick smoothly into all four extreme diagonal corners. This maps the minimum and maximum travel endpoints for **Throttle (Ch 3)** and **Yaw (Ch 4)**.
    • Right Gimbal Sweep (Channels 1 & 2): Push the right stick smoothly into all four extreme diagonal corners. This maps **Roll (Ch 1)** and **Pitch (Ch 2)**.
    • Auxiliary Channels (Channels 5 & 6): On a 6-channel radio, sweep auxiliary knobs and switches to determine their channel mapping:
      • Rotate the auxiliary potentiometer knob through its full travel: observe the Channel 5 green bar move from minimum to maximum.
      • Toggle the auxiliary 2-position switch: observe the Channel 6 bar jump between low and high states.
    • Re-center all primary sticks, pull the throttle stick to its absolute lowest point ($1000\,\mu\text{s}$), and click Click when Done. Click OK to commit the measured endpoints.
  9. Flight Mode Configuration (Step 7): Configure baseline flight modes mapped to your transmitter's auxiliary switch:
    • Set Flight Modes 1 through 5 to `Stabilize`. (Provides manual flight with automatic gyroscopic self-leveling; safe for maiden testing).
    • Set Flight Mode 6 to `Return to Launch` (RTL). (Emergency failsafe mode; triggers autonomous return and landing at the takeoff point).
    • Click Save.
  10. Arming Test & Completion (Step 8): The wizard initiates a pre-arm safety check. Note: The drone may refuse to arm indoors during this test; this is normal behavior because ArduCopter blocks arming when a valid 3D GPS satellite lock is missing indoors or when safety switches are disengaged.
    Leave Geofencing set to disabled. Click Finish. The basic vehicle configuration is complete.

⚠️ Common Problems & Engineering Solutions

Identified Problem Underlying Root Cause Detection Method Engineering Remediation Why the Solution Works
Accelerometer Calibration Times Out / Fails The airframe was disturbed, vibrated, or moved while the algorithm was sampling a gravity vector. Mission Planner displays an error dialog: "Calibration Failed / Timeout". Rest the airframe against solid, flat surfaces. When holding the inverted position, brace your hands firmly and remain completely still before clicking Continue. Static accelerometer calibration requires pure, stationary $1g$ DC gravity vector measurements without dynamic hand-shake noise.
Compass Sampling Hangs / Does Not Fill Sphere The airframe is not being rotated through enough unique 3D spherical orientations. The progress bar in Mission Planner halts below 100% despite continuous spinning along a single axis. Tumble the drone through all three physical planes: do continuous somersaults (Pitch), barrel rolls (Roll), and pirouettes (Yaw). Calculating a 3D soft-iron correction ellipsoid requires data points distributed across the entire upper, lower, and lateral spherical surfaces.
Radio Calibration Bars Do Not Move in GCS RC receiver is unpowered, not bound to the transmitter, or signal wire is unseated from the RC IN port. Transmitter sticks are moved, but green channel indicator bars in Mission Planner remain frozen at $0\,\mu\text{s}$ or $1500\,\mu\text{s}$. Verify receiver LED shows a solid bind light; verify receiver output connects to Pixhawk RC IN (not an output rail). The flight controller's input capture timer requires an active serial data stream (SBUS/PPM) to decode channel pulse widths.
Drone Refuses to Arm at Wizard Conclusion Pre-Arm safety checks reject arming due to missing outdoor GPS satellite lock, disengaged safety switch, or compass interference. Buzzer emits a low error tone; Mission Planner displays: "PreArm: Need 3D Fix" or "PreArm: Safety Switch". Inspect the red annunciator text on the Flight Data HUD screen. Press the physical safety switch until solid red, or move outdoors to achieve a 3D GPS lock. ArduCopter includes strict safety interlocks that block motor arming when flight-critical sensors are unready or degraded.
Transmitter Refuses to Boot Up for RC Calibration Transmitter safety interlock detects that toggle switches or throttle sticks are not in their safe resting states. Transmitter vibrates, emits continuous warning beeps, and displays: "SWITCH WARNING" or "THROTTLE WARNING". Ensure the throttle stick is pulled all the way down, and flip all auxiliary toggle switches up/away into their default positions. EdgeTX/OpenTX firmware enforces pre-start checks to prevent accidental motor spin-up or uncommanded mode changes upon boot.

📚 Technical Terminology

Term Technical Meaning Context & Practical Importance
Setup Wizard An automated, multi-step graphical setup pipeline inside Mission Planner. Guides the builder through foundational frame, sensor, battery, and radio calibrations without manually editing parameter lists.
Frame Type (X-Frame) A multirotor geometry where arms form an symmetric "X" relative to the forward flight vector. Standard layout for camera and FPV drones; keeps propellers clear of front-facing cameras while providing symmetrical pitch/roll authority.
6-Axis Calibration Sampling gravity ($1g$) along six orthogonal directions: Level, Left, Right, Nose Down, Nose Up, and Inverted. Enables the flight controller's IMU to determine absolute horizontal level and calculate tilt angles accurately.
Hard-Iron Distortion Permanent magnetic fields generated by ferromagnetic materials (steel screws, motor magnets, buzzers) mounted on the airframe. Shifts the center of the magnetic sphere away from $(0,0,0)$; must be offset in software to prevent heading errors.
Soft-Iron Distortion Distortions generated by magnetically permeable materials that alter the shape of the surrounding magnetic field. Deforms the spherical magnetic field into an ellipsoid; corrected by software transformation matrices.
PWM Microseconds ($\mu\text{s}$) Pulse Width Modulation duration used to represent analog channel positions in RC systems. Standard RC range spans from $1000\,\mu\text{s}$ (minimum) to $2000\,\mu\text{s}$ (maximum), with $1500\,\mu\text{s}$ as neutral center.
RTL (Return to Launch) An autonomous ArduCopter failsafe flight mode. Commands the drone to climb to a safe return altitude, navigate back to the takeoff coordinate using GPS, and execute an automated landing.
Pre-Arm Checks Automated sensor and software health audits executed by ArduPilot before allowing motor arming. Blocks motor start if sensors are uncalibrated, battery voltage is low, GPS precision is poor, or hardware faults exist.

💡 Practical Takeaways

  • 🔴 Remove All Propellers: Never run the Setup Wizard with propellers attached to the motors. The wizard tests radio control endpoints and prompts for motor arming; bench testing with propellers installed is dangerous.
  • 🟢 Calibrate Compass Wirelessly: Conducting live compass calibration over a wireless telemetry link eliminates USB cable tangles and prevents physical strain on the flight controller's micro-USB port.
  • 🟡 Protect the Elevated GPS Mast: During the inverted (back) step of the accelerometer calibration, do not place the drone flat on the delicate GPS puck. Support the inverted airframe by hand, hold it level, and click continue.
  • 🟢 Radio Calibration Sweeps: Push gimbals into all four diagonal corners rather than just moving them along single axes. This ensures both axes reach their maximum travel limits simultaneously.
  • ⚠️ Indoor Arming Rejection is Normal: If the drone refuses to arm at the end of the wizard, do not assume hardware failure. ArduCopter blocks arming indoors if a valid 3D GPS satellite lock is missing or the physical safety button is not pressed.

❓ Questions & Answers (Self-Assessment)

Q1: Why is an "X" frame configuration preferred over a "Plus (+)" configuration for modern drones?
The "X" configuration rotates the arms $45^\circ$ away from the centerline, keeping propellers clear of front-facing cameras and sensors while providing symmetric, decoupled pitch and roll control dynamics.
Q2: Why must the drone remain completely stationary during each step of the accelerometer calibration?
The calibration routine samples Earth's static $1g$ gravitational field to establish zero-biases and scaling factors. Any physical movement or vibration during sampling injects dynamic acceleration noise into the data, corrupting the horizontal level reference.
Q3: Why is performing compass calibration over wireless telemetry recommended over a direct USB cable?
Compass calibration requires tumbling the airframe $360^\circ$ through all three physical axes. A wired USB connection restricts physical movement, tangles around the frame, and risks bending or breaking the flight controller's USB connector.
Q4: Why does the drone typically refuse to arm during the wizard's final test indoors?
ArduCopter's default pre-arm safety checks enforce strict health criteria. Indoors, the GPS receiver cannot acquire a solid 3D satellite lock with low HDOP, and the external safety switch may not be engaged, causing the autopilot to block motor arming.
Q5: Why is radio calibration required if standard RC control signals are already nominally 1000–2000µs?
Transmitter hardware, gimbals, and protocol variations introduce small endpoint discrepancies (e.g., $1004\,\mu\text{s}$ to $1996\,\mu\text{s}$). Radio calibration maps the transmitter's specific physical stick travel to the flight controller's internal control channels.
