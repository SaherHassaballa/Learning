PID Controller Basics

A PID controller drives a system's error (the difference between a target value, or setpoint, and the measured value) toward zero by combining three terms.

u(t) = Kp·e(t) + Ki·∫e(t)dt + Kd·de(t)/dt
P

Proportional — reacts to the current error. Higher Kp means a faster response but more overshoot and, past a point, oscillation.

I

Integral — accumulates past error over time, eliminating steady-state offset (e.g. a quad that hovers slightly below the commanded altitude). Too much causes slow oscillation ("integral windup").

D

Derivative — reacts to the rate of change of error, damping oscillation and smoothing the response. Sensitive to sensor noise, so it's often paired with a low-pass filter in practice.

Tuning order for a flight controller loop

  1. Start with all gains at zero (or firmware defaults) and increase P until the axis responds crisply but starts to oscillate — then back off ~20–30%.
  2. Add D to damp the oscillation you just saw appear.
  3. Add a small amount of I to remove any steady offset, watching for slow "wallowing" oscillation, which means I is too high.
  4. Retest at different throttle/airspeed points — gains that feel right at hover can be wrong at high speed.
