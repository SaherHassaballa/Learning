Introduction to ArduPilot

ArduPilot is an open-source autopilot firmware supporting a very wide range of vehicles: multirotors, fixed-wing planes, VTOL, rovers, boats, and submarines. It began as an Arduino project and has grown into one of the most capable and widely deployed autopilot stacks in the world, running on everything from hobby quads to research and commercial platforms.

Where it fits in the stack

A flight stack is generally split into two layers: the flight controller firmware (ArduPilot itself, running on the flight controller board) and the ground control station software you use to configure and monitor it (commonly Mission Planner or QGroundControl).

Vehicle firmwares

ArduPilot ships separate firmware builds per vehicle type — you flash the one that matches your airframe:

  • ArduCopter — multirotors and traditional helicopters
  • ArduPlane — fixed-wing aircraft and VTOL hybrids
  • Rover — ground vehicles
  • ArduSub — underwater vehicles
Note: the firmware you choose determines which parameters and flight modes are available — always match it to your actual airframe before your first calibration pass.

What's next

Once firmware is flashed, the next step is sensor calibration (accelerometer, compass, and radio) before any flight testing. See the Sensor Calibration note for the full walkthrough.
