🎥 Servo Control/Channel Forwarding in ArduPilot

Source: YouTube - Painless360 Duration: 3m 49s Instructor: Painless360 Main Topic: Channel Forwarding (RC Pass-Through), Servo Output Function Mapping, and Servo Rail Power Isolation in ArduPilot

🎯 What This Video Is About

This tutorial explains how to actuate an auxiliary physical servo (such as a payload drop release, retractable landing gear, pan-and-tilt camera rig, or cargo hatch) directly from an auxiliary switch on a handheld radio transmitter through an ArduPilot flight controller (demonstrated on a Pixhawk Cube on a Mini Carrier Board).

Because modern aircraft use serialized digital receiver protocols (S.Bus, CRSF, or ELRS) over a single 3-pin lead into the RC IN port, individual receiver servo pins are no longer accessible. The autopilot must act as an intelligent signal router by executing Channel Forwarding (RC Pass-Through). The video covers identifying incoming transmitter channels in Mission Planner, assigning the destination hardware pin via Servo Output parameters, tuning servo endpoints/midpoints, and solving the critical servo rail electrical power isolation boundary using an external Battery Eliminator Circuit (BEC).

🧠 Core Concepts

1. Channel Forwarding (RC Pass-Through)
What is it? A software routing routine inside ArduPilot that intercepts a specific incoming digital channel from the serial receiver stream (e.g., Channel 5 commanded by switch SG) and forwards its raw pulse width directly to an assigned physical output header pin (e.g., Output 8).
Why do we use it? Modern RC receivers transmit up to 16 digital channels across a single wire into RC IN. Without individual PWM pins on the receiver, peripheral actuators must be routed through the flight controller's physical output rail.
How does it work? By setting the destination output parameter SERVOx_FUNCTION to the designated pass-through function (such as RCIN5 or function value 55), ArduPilot bypasses its flight stabilization PID algorithms for that pin and directly mirrors the pilot's switch deflection.
Where is it used? Payload drop hooks, retractable landing gear, camera gimbal pan/tilt servos, nose-wheel steering, glider tow releases, and aerial searchlights.
Prerequisites: A bound multi-channel transmitter/receiver, active digital communication over RC IN, and completed Radio Calibration in Mission Planner.
2. The Servo Rail Power Isolation Boundary
What is it? The physical galvanic and electrical isolation between the Pixhawk's internal $5.3\,\text{V}$ processor logic bus and the center positive pin rail ($+5\,\text{V}$) of the MAIN OUT and AUX OUT headers.
Why do we study it? Servos draw inductive motor current, generate back-EMF voltage spikes, and can pull heavy stall currents ($1\,\text{A} - 3\,\text{A}+$ per servo). If servos were powered from the flight computer's internal MCU power supply, an overloaded servo would trigger a catastrophic CPU brownout and immediate in-flight system reboot.
How does it work? Powering the Pixhawk via USB or the primary Power Module ($5.3\,\text{V}$) energizes only the STM32 microcontroller, internal sensors, and the RC IN header. The center positive pin along the servo rail remains completely unpowered ($0.0\,\text{V}$). An external $5\,\text{V} - 6\,\text{V}$ BEC (from an ESC or dedicated UBEC) must be connected to the rail to provide servo operating current.
The Engineering Fix: Inject $5\,\text{V}$ power onto the servo rail using an ESC's built-in BEC or wire an external standalone UBEC to the output rail.
3. Output Calibration Registers (SERVOx_MIN, MAX, TRIM)
What is it? Microsecond ($\mu\text{s}$) pulse-width limit registers in ArduPilot that govern the physical throw boundaries and neutral rest position of an assigned servo channel.
Why do we use it? Servos have strict mechanical stops. If the autopilot outputs pulse durations exceeding an actuator's mechanical range, the motor will stall against internal stops, drawing peak current, heating up, and stripping plastic/metal drive gears.
How does it work? By adjusting SERVOx_MIN (typically $1000\,\mu\text{s} - 1100\,\mu\text{s}$), SERVOx_MAX (typically $1900\,\mu\text{s} - 2000\,\mu\text{s}$), and SERVOx_TRIM ($1500\,\mu\text{s}$), the builder clamps the travel envelope to match the physical linkage geometry.
Where is it used? Mechanical pushrods, drop-pin releases, and camera mount rotation limits.

🔧 Technical Details: Electrical Architecture & Signal Routing

Channel forwarding requires establishing a clean data and power separation across the autopilot interfaces. The table below details the hardware parameters and electrical conditions:

Interface / Channel Electrical / Logical Specification Operational Function in Channel Forwarding Power Source & Rail Status

Auxiliary Servo Micro $9\,\text{g}$ or Standard Actuator ($1000 - 2000\,\mu\text{s}$ pulse range) Actuates physical mechanism (drops payload, deploys landing gear, rotates camera). Powered exclusively via the external BEC rail; consumes $< 50\,\text{mA}$ idle, up to $1.5\,\text{A}$ stall.
🔴
The Servo Power Starvation Trap:
Plugging a servo into Output 8 (or any other MAIN or AUX output pin) while the Pixhawk is connected to USB or the Power Module will result in a completely limp servo arm. The USB bus and the Power Module do not supply voltage to the center pin (+5V) of the output rail. Without an external BEC connected to the rail, the servo receives signal pulses but zero operating voltage.
⚠️
The Parameter Value Trap: RCPassThru (1) vs. RCINx (51–66):
In older or advanced ArduPilot parameter tables, setting SERVOx_FUNCTION = 1 activates generic RCPassThru, which maps the identical numerical input channel (e.g., Output 8 passes through Input 8). To explicitly forward a specific channel regardless of output index (e.g., forwarding Input Channel 5 to physical Output 8), you must select RCIN5 (Parameter integer value: 55).

🛠️ Practical Implementation: Step-by-Step Configuration Workflow

Follow this exact sequence to configure and test channel forwarding safely on the bench:

Phase 1: Transmitter Switch Mapping & Verification

  1. Assign Switch in Transmitter: On your handheld radio (OpenTX, EdgeTX, FrSky, Spektrum, RadioMaster), enter the MIXER screen. Assign a hardware switch (such as SG or SA) to an unassigned auxiliary channel (in Painless360's demonstration, Channel 5).
  2. Connect to Ground Station: Connect the Pixhawk to your PC using a direct USB cable. Launch Mission Planner and click CONNECT ($115200\text{ baud}$).
  3. Audit Incoming Signal in Radio Calibration: Navigate to: Setup -> Mandatory Hardware -> Radio Calibration. Flip switch SG back and forth. Confirm that the green calibration bar for Radio 5 moves smoothly between $1000\,\mu\text{s}$ (bottom) and $2000\,\mu\text{s}$ (top). Note the channel number.

Phase 2: Assigning the Output Function in Mission Planner

  1. Navigate to Servo Output Menu: Go to: Setup -> Mandatory Hardware -> Servo Output.
  2. Assign Destination Output Pin: Locate the physical header pin where your servo is plugged in (Painless360 uses Output 8). Click the dropdown menu next to Output 8 and select:
    RCIN5 (or search for SERVO8_FUNCTION in the Full Parameter Tree and set it to 55).
  3. Real-Time Diagnostic Verification: While looking at the Servo Output screen, flip transmitter switch SG. You will see the numeric indicator and the green slider bar for Output 8 jump dynamically in real time between the minimum ($1000\,\mu\text{s}$) and maximum ($2000\,\mu\text{s}$) positions.

Phase 3: Hardware Power Injection & Live Actuator Testing

  1. Safety Check: Remove Propellers! If an ESC or motor is connected to the vehicle, ensure all propellers are removed before powering the system with a battery.
  2. Connect the Auxiliary Servo: Plug the servo connector into the Output 8 header pins. Ensure proper orientation:
    • Signal Pin (White or Orange): Faces the signal row (bottom on Pixhawk Cube carrier boards).
    • Positive Pin (Red): Connects to the center row.
    • Ground Pin (Black or Brown): Connects to the ground row (top).
  3. Inject External 5V Power: Connect an external $5\,\text{V}$ BEC (either the throttle lead from a BEC-equipped ESC on another output pin, or a standalone UBEC plugged into an unused header slot such as Output 7). The servo will emit a brief mechanical jump and hold its position under torque.
  4. Tune Physical Travel & Endpoints: In the Servo Output tab, adjust the Min (e.g., $1100\,\mu\text{s}$), Max (e.g., $1900\,\mu\text{s}$), and Trim ($1500\,\mu\text{s}$) values to ensure the servo moves the drop mechanism or landing gear through its full physical stroke without mechanical binding or gear buzzing.

💻 Parameters, Mixer Registers & Technical Values

Parameter Identifier Standard Value / Setting Functional Engineering Context
SERVO8_FUNCTION 55 (RCIN5) Assigns physical Output 8 pin to mirror incoming pilot command from RC Channel 5.
SERVOx_FUNCTION 51 to 66 Generic pass-through parameters corresponding explicitly to RCIN1 through RCIN16.
SERVOx_FUNCTION 1 (RCPassThru) Generic 1:1 pass-through (Output $x$ forwards Input $x$). Can cause confusion if input channel differs from output pin.
SERVOx_MIN $1000\,\mu\text{s} - 1100\,\mu\text{s}$ Clamps the lowest PWM pulse width emitted on that pin to prevent linkage over-travel.
SERVOx_MAX $1900\,\mu\text{s} - 2000\,\mu\text{s}$ Clamps the highest PWM pulse width emitted on that pin to prevent servo stall.
SERVOx_TRIM $1500\,\mu\text{s}$ nominal The neutral pulse width sent to the servo when the incoming channel rests at center.
SERVOx_REVERSED 0 (Normal) or 1 (Inverted) Flips the mechanical actuation direction of the servo without altering transmitter configuration.

⚠️ Common Problems & Engineering Solutions

Identified Fault Symptom Root Cause Diagnostic Identification Engineering Remediation Why the Solution Works
Servo Remains Completely Limp & Motionless The center positive rail (+5V) is unpowered because the Pixhawk is only connected via USB or Power Module. Mission Planner HUD reflects switch motion and Servo Output bars move, but the servo horn has zero mechanical resistance. Plug an external 5V BEC (from an ESC or dedicated UBEC) into any unused positive/ground pin on the output rail. Energizes the isolated output power bus independently from the sensitive MCU logic supply, delivering operating current to the servo motor.
Wrong Switch Controls the Servo The selected SERVOx_FUNCTION parameter does not match the channel assigned to the switch in the transmitter mixer. Moving Switch SG does not change the servo, but moving another switch or stick does. Open Radio Calibration, toggle the switch, note the exact channel number that moves, and set SERVOx_FUNCTION to that RCINx value. Corrects the internal software routing matrix to link the target incoming channel to the physical output pin.
Servo Buzzes, Strains, or Draws Excessive Current The commanded PWM pulse range ($1000 - 2000\,\mu\text{s}$) drives the servo horn beyond its physical mechanical limits. Servo emits a loud, high-pitch continuous hum at the end of travel; pushrod flexes; servo gets warm to the touch. In the Servo Output screen, raise the Min (e.g., to $1150\,\mu\text{s}$) and lower the Max (e.g., to $1850\,\mu\text{s}$) until buzzing stops. Restricts the electrical command envelope to match the mechanical stroke of the physical linkage, preventing motor stall and gear stripping.
Servo Operates Backwards from Desired Switch Position Mechanical linkage geometry is inverted relative to standard PWM timing polarity. Flipping switch up closes the drop hook instead of opening it. Check the Reverse checkbox next to that output in Mission Planner's Servo Output tab (or set SERVOx_REVERSED = 1). Inverts output PWM pulse timing in software without requiring messy channel reversals in the handheld transmitter.

📚 Technical Terminology

Term Technical Definition Context & Practical Importance
Channel Forwarding Routing an incoming control channel from the receiver directly to a designated hardware output pin. Allows auxiliary pilot commands (switches/knobs) to bypass autonomous flight stabilization and control mechanisms directly.
RC Pass-Through The ArduPilot software operating mode (RCINx) where output PWM pulse width mirrors incoming RC pulse width. The core mechanism used to actuate secondary mechanisms without requiring custom programming or dedicated companion computers.
BEC (Battery Eliminator Circuit) A voltage regulator circuit stepping down high LiPo battery voltage ($11.1\text{V} - 25.2\text{V}$) to $+5.0\text{V}$. Essential for powering the isolated servo power rail on the Pixhawk flight controller.
Galvanic / Power Isolation The physical separation of electrical power circuits to prevent current flow between distinct subsystems. Protects the Pixhawk's internal STM32 CPU and IMUs from inductive voltage spikes and stall-current brownouts caused by servos.
SERVOx_FUNCTION The primary ArduPilot configuration parameter governing the behavior and routing logic of output pin $x$. Configures pins for motor throttle, flight control surfaces (ailerons/elevators), or auxiliary pass-through (RCINx).

💡 Practical Takeaways

  • 🔴 Servos Will Never Move on USB Power: Never troubleshoot an unresponsive servo connected to a Pixhawk while powering the board solely through USB. USB powers only the CPU and receiver; the servo rail center positive pin remains completely dead until energized by an external 5V BEC.
  • 🟢 Identify the Channel First: Before touching servo parameters, always open Initial Setup -> Mandatory Hardware -> Radio Calibration, toggle your target switch, and observe which green bar moves to confirm the incoming channel number.
  • 🟡 Use Explicit RCINx Functions: Avoid setting generic RCPassThru (value 1) if your input channel does not match your output pin number. Use explicit parameters such as RCIN5 (value 55) to route Channel 5 to Output 8.
  • ⚠️ Audit Mechanical Travel Limits: Always test new servo linkages by hand before powering up. Once powered, ensure the servo does not buzz or strain against physical stops by adjusting Min and Max pulse widths in Mission Planner.
  • 🔴 Remove Propellers During Bench Setup: Even though you are configuring auxiliary servos, connecting a flight battery to power an ESC BEC can accidentally spin motors if throttle failsafes trigger. Keep propellers off at all times.

📝 Quick Revision (High-Yield Facts)

  • The Power Rule: Only the RC IN receiver port receives $+5\text{V}$ from internal Pixhawk logic power. The MAIN OUT and AUX OUT positive rails receive zero power by default.
  • Channel Forwarding Mapping: In Mission Planner's Servo Output screen, set the destination pin to RCINx (where $x$ matches the channel toggled by your transmitter switch).
  • Parameter Numerical Values: In the Full Parameter Tree, RCIN1 through RCIN16 correspond to parameter integer values 51 through 66.
  • Reversing Direction: Reverse backwards servo travel by checking the Reverse checkbox in Mission Planner's Servo Output menu, never on the transmitter.
  • Live Monitoring: Mission Planner displays real-time slider bars on the Servo Output screen, allowing you to verify software routing before connecting physical hardware.

❓ Questions & Answers (Self-Assessment)

Q1: Why does a servo connected to a Pixhawk remain completely unresponsive when the board is powered via USB?
The Pixhawk features intentional electrical isolation between its internal microcontroller logic supply and the external servo rail header. USB power energizes only the STM32 processor, internal IMU sensors, and the RC IN port. The center positive pin (+5V) along the MAIN and AUX output rails receives zero voltage to protect the sensitive flight computer from inductive voltage spikes and stall-current brownouts caused by servos. An external 5V BEC must be connected to power the rail.
Q2: How do you identify which RC channel is assigned to a physical switch on your transmitter?
Connect the Pixhawk to Mission Planner, navigate to Initial Setup -> Mandatory Hardware -> Radio Calibration, and physically toggle the switch on the transmitter. Watch the green graphical indicator bars move across the screen; the bar that jumps between $1000\,\mu\text{s}$ and $2000\,\mu\text{s}$ identifies the active incoming channel number (e.g., Channel 5).
Q3: What is the difference between setting SERVOx_FUNCTION to 1 (RCPassThru) versus setting it to 55 (RCIN5)?
Setting SERVOx_FUNCTION = 1 (generic RCPassThru) mirrors the identical numerical channel (for example, physical Output 8 will attempt to mirror incoming Channel 8). If the pilot's switch is assigned to Channel 5, the servo on Output 8 will not move. Setting SERVOx_FUNCTION = 55 (explicit RCIN5) directs physical Output 8 to forward incoming Channel 5 regardless of pin index.
Q4: How do you prevent a forwarded servo from mechanically binding against internal stops?
In Mission Planner's Servo Output screen, locate the destination output pin and adjust the Min and Max microsecond limit fields. Narrowing the pulse range (e.g., raising Min from $1000\,\mu\text{s}$ to $1150\,\mu\text{s}$ and lowering Max from $2000\,\mu\text{s}$ to $1850\,\mu\text{s}$) restricts physical actuator travel to match the mechanical stroke of the linkage.
Q5: Where should servo reversing be configured if the actuator moves backwards relative to the switch?
Servo reversing should be configured exclusively inside Mission Planner by checking the Reverse checkbox for that specific output pin in the Servo Output menu (or setting SERVOx_REVERSED = 1 in parameters). Reversing auxiliary channels on the transmitter should be avoided to maintain clean, standardized radio profiles.
