🎯 What This Video Is About

This tutorial details the mandatory benchtop commissioning procedure that must occur before physically installing a Pixhawk (specifically demonstrated with a Pixhawk 2.1 Cube Black) into an airframe. Performing firmware flashing, receiver binding, and initial sensor baselining on an open workbench ensures that any software defects, communication timeouts, or hardware misconfigurations are diagnosed in isolation, eliminating the frustration of troubleshooting inside a cramped fuselage.

The video guides the viewer through wiping legacy Ground Control Station (GCS) driver conflicts, configuring a raw 4-channel radio model without onboard mixing, navigating the disconnected firmware flasher inside Mission Planner, and executing the mandatory hardware calibrations: 6-axis orthogonal accelerometer sampling, 3D live spherical compass calibration, and radio control endpoint normalization.

🧠 Core Concepts

1. The Zero-Mixer Radio Principle (Autopilot Aerodynamic Authority)
What is it? A strict configuration rule mandating that the RC transmitter output pure, unmixed stick commands on Channels 1 through 4 (Aileron, Elevator, Throttle, Rudder), even when the target aircraft is an elevon flying wing or a V-tail.
Why do we use it? If mixing is programmed inside the handheld radio transmitter, the autopilot receives compounded mathematical inputs. ArduPlane requires raw demand setpoints from the pilot so its internal cascaded PID controllers can calculate vehicle attitude errors and dynamically mix control surface actuations to provide active stabilization and flight autonomy.
How does it work? The transmitter outputs discrete single-axis deflections over S.Bus or CRSF. ArduPlane's mixer matrix (configured later in SERVOx_FUNCTION) takes the pitch and roll demands, combines them with internal gyroscopic rate damping, and drives the physical servos.
Where is it used? All fixed-wing and VTOL aircraft governed by autonomous flight controllers (ArduPlane, PX4, iNav).
Example: On a V-tail aircraft, the pilot pulls the pitch stick back. If the radio has V-tail mixing active, it commands Ch 1 and Ch 2 simultaneously. The autopilot misinterprets this as combined roll and pitch requests, producing severe control instability.
Prerequisites: Basic familiarity with radio channel assignments (AETR vs. TAER).
2. Disconnected Bootloader Flashing Architecture
What is it? The software requirement that firmware flashing must be executed while Mission Planner is disconnected from active MAVLink communications.
Why do we use it? An active MAVLink telemetry session continuously queries the autopilot's main flight loop over serial. The microcontroller's internal bootloader ROM cannot safely erase flash sectors and stream a new operating system binary while an active telemetry session locks the serial port.
How does it work? When the user selects the target firmware in Mission Planner, the GCS issues a soft-reboot command to the board. The STM32 processor resets into its native bootloader. The flasher erases old flash memory, writes the compiled ArduPlane binary, and verifies checksums before the main flight code boots.
Where is it used? Initial board bring-up, major version updates, or cross-flashing between airframe types (e.g., ArduCopter to ArduPlane).
Prerequisites: Correctly registered STM32 / PX4 Virtual COM Port (VCP) drivers.
3. ArduPlane Channel 2 (Elevator) Negative Logic
What is it? An architectural quirk in ArduPlane's radio input mapping where pushing the pitch stick forward (commanding aircraft nose-down / dive) expects a lower microsecond ($\mu\text{s}$) PWM value, contrary to standard radio manufacturer conventions.
Why do we study it? Most stock radio transmitters output a higher pulse width ($> 1500\,\mu\text{s}$) when the elevator stick is pushed forward. If not inverted on the transmitter, ArduPlane interprets forward stick as a command to pull the nose up into an aerodynamic stall.
The Engineering Fix: Reversing Channel 2 (Elevator) strictly inside the transmitter's output/servo menu prior to conducting the initial radio calibration in Mission Planner.

🔴
The Direct PC USB Connection Mandate:
Never connect the Pixhawk to your computer through an unpowered external USB hub, keyboard pass-through, or front-chassis splitter during firmware flashing. Voltage drops or packet framing collisions across shared hubs can interrupt the STM32 bootloader mid-write, resulting in a soft-bricked microcontroller that requires hardware DFU recovery. Always plug directly into a native motherboard USB port.
⚠️
The Servo Rail Power Isolation Boundary:
Connecting the USB cable energizes the internal microcontroller, internal sensors, and the RC IN receiver header. It does not deliver power to the center positive rail (+5V) of MAIN OUT (1–8) or AUX OUT (1–6). Do not plug servos into the output rail expecting them to operate under USB power alone. Servos remain limp until energized by an external 5V BEC.

🛠️ Practical Implementation: Step-by-Step Commissioning Roadmap

Follow this exact chronological sequence to commission the electronics on the bench:

Phase 1: Driver Hygiene & Workstation Preparation

  1. Purge Legacy Drivers: If legacy APM 2.5/2.6 boards or old Mission Planner versions were previously used, perform a clean uninstall. Old FTDI virtual COM port drivers can conflict with modern 32-bit STM32 composite drivers.
  2. Fresh Mission Planner Installation: Download and install the latest stable .msi build from the official ArduPilot portal. During installation, accept all Windows User Account Control (UAC) prompts to inject the certified PX4, STM32 Bootloader, and Silicon Labs device drivers.

Phase 2: Handheld Transmitter Baseline Configuration

  1. Create a Clean Model: In your transmitter (EdgeTX, OpenTX, FrSky, RadioMaster), create a fresh profile. Select a standard fixed-wing aircraft format (1 Aileron, 1 Elevator, 1 Throttle, 1 Rudder). Do not enable elevon, V-tail, or delta mixing.
  2. Verify Channel Order (AETR):
    • Channel 1: Aileron (Roll Axis)
    • Channel 2: Elevator (Pitch Axis) $\rightarrow$ Set to REVERSED in transmitter outputs.
    • Channel 3: Throttle (Propulsion Axis)
    • Channel 4: Rudder (Yaw Axis)
  3. Assign Flight Mode Switch: Assign a physical 3-position toggle switch to Channel 8 (standard default for ArduPlane mode selection).
  4. Bind Receiver: Bind the receiver to the transmitter on the bench.

Phase 3: Firmware Flashing (The Disconnected Workflow)

  1. Hardware Layout: Connect the external GPS/compass puck to the GPS 1 port, ensuring the puck's forward arrow points parallel to the Pixhawk's forward arrow. Connect the receiver to RC IN. Leave motor ESCs, telemetry radios, and battery leads completely disconnected.
  2. Launch Mission Planner: Open Mission Planner. Do NOT click the green "CONNECT" button in the upper right corner.
  3. Initiate Firmware Flasher: Navigate to: Initial Setup -> Install Firmware. Click the Plane airframe icon (ArduPlane official release, e.g., Plane 4.0.x).
  4. Select Hardware Target: When prompted with board variants, select CubeBlack (or the exact silicon variant corresponding to your board).
  5. Execute Bootloader Re-enumeration: Mission Planner will download the hex binary and display: "Please unplug the board, click OK, plug back in". Unplug the USB cable from the Pixhawk, click OK, and plug the USB cable back in within 5 seconds.
  6. Automated Flash & Verification: The flasher detects the STM32 bootloader, wipes the internal EEPROM, writes the new ArduPlane operating system, and verifies data integrity. Upon completion, the autopilot emits an audible musical initialization chime.

Phase 4: Establishing MAVLink & Sensor Calibrations

  1. Connect Telemetry: In the upper-right corner of Mission Planner, select the active virtual COM port (e.g., COM3 - CubeBlack) and set the baud rate strictly to 115200. Click CONNECT. Parameter tables will populate across the link.
  2. HUD Sanity Check: Go to the Flight Data screen. Tilt the board physically. Confirm the artificial horizon tracks the motion in real time.
  3. 6-Axis Accelerometer Calibration: Navigate to: Initial Setup -> Mandatory Hardware -> Accel Calibration. Click Calibrate Accelerometer. Sequentially place the unit in 6 orthogonal positions:
    • Level: Flat on workbench, top facing up $\rightarrow$ Click when done.
    • Left: Resting squarely on its left edge $\rightarrow$ Click when done.
    • Right: Resting squarely on its right edge $\rightarrow$ Click when done.
    • Nose Down: Vertical with arrow pointing straight down $\rightarrow$ Click when done.
    • Nose Up: Vertical with arrow pointing straight up $\rightarrow$ Click when done.
    • On Back: Completely inverted, top facing down $\rightarrow$ Click when done.
    ⚠️
    Stationary Sampling Rule: Ensure the flight controller is resting completely motionless on a solid surface before pressing each confirmation key. Hand tremors or table vibrations inject linear acceleration noise into the EKF bias calculations. Note: Calibrate Level is skipped during bench setup and performed after the unit is permanently installed in the airframe.
  4. Live 3D Compass Calibration: Navigate to: Mandatory Hardware -> Compass. Click Live Calibration. Pick up both the flight controller and the GPS puck together (keeping their orientation arrows parallel). Slowly rotate and tumble the assembly in continuous 360° spheres across roll, pitch, and yaw axes. Data points populate the spherical sampling matrix. Once the progress bars fill, click OK and power-cycle the board.
  5. Radio Control Calibration: Navigate to: Mandatory Hardware -> Radio Calibration. Power on the bound RC transmitter. Click Calibrate Radio. Move both gimbals simultaneously to their extreme diagonal corners to register maximum travel boundaries. Flip the Channel 8 mode switch through all 3 positions. Ensure:
    • Roll (Ch 1): Left = $\sim 1000\,\mu\text{s}$, Right = $\sim 2000\,\mu\text{s}$
    • Pitch (Ch 2): Forward/Down = $\sim 1000\,\mu\text{s}$, Back/Up = $\sim 2000\,\mu\text{s}$ (Verified inverted)
    • Throttle (Ch 3): Bottom = $\sim 1000\,\mu\text{s}$, Top = $\sim 2000\,\mu\text{s}$
    • Yaw (Ch 4): Left = $\sim 1000\,\mu\text{s}$, Right = $\sim 2000\,\mu\text{s}$
    Pull throttle to zero, center all other sticks, and click Click when Done to commit limits.

Firmware Upload Fails:
Communication Error / No Heartbeat		 Attempting to flash firmware while an active MAVLink telemetry session is running, or using an unpowered USB hub. Mission Planner displays: Calibration Failed: Accels inconsistent. Rest the board flat against a solid workbench block. Keep hands entirely off the board before pressing confirmation keys. Allows the sensor fusion filter to compute pure $1g$ static gravitational baselines without dynamic inertial noise.

Compass calibration progress bars fill unevenly; offsets fail tolerance limits. Verify the GPS puck arrow is oriented parallel to the flight controller arrow. Calibrate away from metal benches. Ensures all active tri-axial magnetic sensor suites resolve a shared heading datum relative to Earth's geomagnetic field.

Green radio channel bars in Mission Planner remain completely stationary at $0\,\mu\text{s}$ when gimbals move. Ensure receiver signal is plugged strictly into RC IN (not SBUS OUT). Verify transmitter is powered and bound. RC IN routes through internal hardware inverters directly to the STM32 capture registers and supplies USB bus 5V power.

📚 Technical Terminology

Term Technical Definition Context & Practical Importance
Bootloader A specialized, lightweight program executing from protected microcontroller ROM immediately upon system reset. Initializes hardware peripherals, verifies firmware checksums, and enables memory flashing without requiring external JTAG programmers.
DFU (Device Firmware Upgrade) An official USB protocol standard enabling microcontrollers to download and execute firmware directly into flash memory. The mechanism used by Mission Planner to recover or re-flash raw STM32 chips over a standard USB cable.
AETR Standard channel assignment: Ch 1 = Aileron, Ch 2 = Elevator, Ch 3 = Throttle, Ch 4 = Rudder. The default channel sequence expected by ArduPilot, Betaflight, and modern serial receiver protocols.
S.Bus (Serial Bus) A high-speed, single-wire digital serial communication protocol developed by Futaba transmitting up to 16 channels over UART. Eliminates individual multi-pin PWM servo cable bundles, routing all pilot stick commands over a single 3-pin lead into RC IN.
Carrier Board An external interface PCB that routes the internal high-density Hirose connectors of the Pixhawk Cube to standard external ports. Houses power distribution traces, port isolation circuitry, and physical JST-GH headers for sensors and telemetry.
VCP (Virtual COM Port) A software driver mechanism emulating a hardware RS-232 serial communication port over a high-speed USB interface. Enables the computer operating system to communicate with the STM32 microcontroller using standard COM port serial terminal routines.

💡 Practical Takeaways

  • 🟢 Perform Driver Housekeeping First: Always uninstall legacy Mission Planner or APM 2.x drivers before connecting a modern Pixhawk. Fresh drivers prevent communication hangs during bootloader uploads.
  • 🔴 Never Enable Radio Mixing: Even if your plane is an elevon flying wing or a V-tail, program your radio transmitter as a standard conventional airplane. All aerodynamic surface mixing must be executed inside ArduPlane.
  • 🟡 Always Invert Channel 2: Invert the elevator channel inside your transmitter outputs before conducting radio calibration so that pushing the pitch stick forward outputs a lower microsecond value ($1000\,\mu\text{s}$).
  • 🟢 Keep the Board Motionless During Accel Checks: When prompted to place the vehicle in each of the 6 positions, ensure the flight controller is resting completely motionless on a rigid surface before clicking confirmation keys.
  • ⚠️ Keep Orientations Parallel: Ensure the directional arrow on the external GPS/compass puck points in the exact same heading as the arrow on the Pixhawk case during bench compass calibration.
  • 🔴 Servos Will Not Move on USB Power: Remember that USB power energizes only the internal microcontroller and the RC IN pins. The servo rail center positive pin (+5V) remains dead until energized by an external ESC BEC.

📝 Quick Revision (High-Yield Facts)

  • Flashing Rule: Firmware installation inside Mission Planner must be initiated while disconnected from active MAVLink sessions.
  • Direct USB Connection: Unpowered USB hubs cause voltage sags that can corrupt firmware flashing; connect directly to motherboard USB ports.
  • Channel 8 Allocation: ArduPlane hardcodes incoming Channel 8 as the default flight mode selection channel.
  • Accelerometer Positions: Six orthogonal orientations required: Level, Left side, Right side, Nose Down, Nose Up, and Back/Inverted.
  • Level Calibration Timing: "Calibrate Level" is intentionally skipped on the bench and performed only after the flight controller is permanently mounted in the airframe.
  • Compass Redundancy: The Pixhawk 2.1 Cube Black features triple internal magnetometers plus the external puck magnetometer.

❓ Questions & Answers (Self-Assessment)

Q1: Why must the handheld transmitter be configured without any elevon or V-tail mixing?
ArduPlane relies on receiving pure, raw pilot intent (single-axis demands for pitch, roll, and yaw). If the radio mixes these channels, the autopilot receives compounded signals. It cannot differentiate between human stick input and aerodynamic perturbations, causing internal PID stabilization loops to fight the control surfaces and induce an immediate crash.
Q2: Why must Channel 2 (Elevator) be reversed in the transmitter for ArduPlane?
ArduPlane adheres to an internal mathematical convention where pushing the pitch stick forward (commanding a dive / nose-down attitude) expects a low PWM value ($1000\,\mu\text{s}$), whereas pulling back expects a high value ($2000\,\mu\text{s}$). Most standard radio transmitters output high PWM when pushed forward, requiring channel inversion on the radio to match ArduPlane's expected logic.
Q3: What causes firmware uploads in Mission Planner to hang at "Detecting Board"?
This typically occurs when the user clicks the green "CONNECT" button prior to flashing, establishing an active MAVLink serial session that locks the COM port, or when an unpowered USB hub causes voltage sags that prevent the STM32 chip from entering bootloader ROM mode.
Q4: Why is "Calibrate Level" skipped during the benchtop accelerometer calibration?
"Calibrate Accelerometer" samples the static $1g$ gravity vector across six orthogonal planes to calibrate the sensor's physical scale and bias. "Calibrate Level" sets the vehicle's true aerodynamic cruising datum ($0^\circ$ pitch and roll), which can only be established once the autopilot is physically mounted inside the assembled airframe with the wings propped at their design Angle of Attack.
Q5: Why does the RC receiver power up during bench testing, but connected servos do not?
The Pixhawk's internal USB logic power bus energizes the core processor, internal IMUs, and the RC IN pin headers for bench configuration. However, the center positive pin (+5V) of the MAIN and AUX servo rails is electrically isolated to protect the CPU from inductive servo noise and brownouts, requiring an external 5V BEC to supply servo power.
