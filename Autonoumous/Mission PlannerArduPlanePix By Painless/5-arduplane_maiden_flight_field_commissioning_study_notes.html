The instructor transitions from indoor bench configuration to active airfield testing, demonstrating how to systematically de-risk a first flight.

🧠 Core Concepts

1. The Maiden Flight Objective & Mental Paradigm
What is it? A disciplined, controlled flight test experiment engineered strictly to confirm aerodynamic airworthiness, sensor integrity, and autopilot stabilization loops, rather than executing aerobatics or complex autonomous missions.
Why do we use it? An aircraft on the bench is operating in an idealized, vibration-free, non-aerodynamic environment. The maiden flight subjects the airframe, Extended Kalman Filter (EKF), control surface linkages, and center of gravity to real aerodynamic loads, identifying subtle calibration or mechanical errors safely.
How does it work? The flight is conducted within a conservative, stepped envelope: launching in stabilized mode (FBWA), checking manual control authority, testing a localized GPS orbit (Circle/Loiter), observing cruise dynamics, and landing without entering high-risk autonomous logic.
Where is it used? All fixed-wing, multirotor, VTOL, and commercial UAV commissioning workflows.
Example: Hand-launching the Nano Talon into the wind while holding the transmitter sticks neutral, allowing the autopilot's gyro-accel loop to hold the climb path cleanly.
Prerequisites: Completed 6-axis accelerometer calibration, compass calibration, and physical control surface direction verification.
2. Fly-By-Wire A (FBWA) Launch Dynamics
What is it? ArduPlane's baseline electronically stabilized flight mode, where the autopilot maintains vehicle attitude (pitch and roll) proportionally to transmitter stick deflection, with hard software clamping on maximum bank and pitch angles.
Why do we use it? Hand-launching a low-airspeed fixed-wing pusher model often induces a sharp, destabilizing roll due to motor torque reaction ($\tau_{\text{motor}}$). In manual mode, human latency often results in a wing-tip stall. In FBWA, the autopilot senses roll acceleration within milliseconds and applies instantaneous counter-aileron to maintain level wings.
How does it work? Releasing cyclic sticks to center ($1500\,\mu\text{s}$) commands an automatic level flight attitude. Full cyclic deflection commands maximum bank angle (governed by LIM_ROLL_CD). Throttle remains under direct, unassisted pilot control.
Where is it used? Hand-launches, bungee launches, initial maiden sorties, and calm scale cruising.
Example: The spotter throws the model firmly forward; the model rolls $15^\circ$ right from motor torque, but ArduPlane deflects the left aileron downward and right aileron upward instantly, leveling the aircraft as it climbs.
Relationship to other concepts: Acts as the intermediate layer between raw direct control (MANUAL) and autonomous GPS-guided navigation (AUTO / LOITER).
3. Positive Angle of Attack (AoA) & Attitude Level Calibration
What is it? The pitch offset introduced during the accelerometer level calibration (Calibrate Level) to ensure the airframe flies with a slightly nose-up pitch attitude relative to the physical horizon.
Why do we use it? Symmetrical or semi-symmetrical airfoils generate zero or insufficient lift when moving through the air at exactly $0^\circ$ pitch ($L = \frac{1}{2}\rho v^2 S C_L$). An aircraft calibrated dead-level on a flat bench will hold the fuselage parallel to the ground in FBWA, causing it to bleed altitude steadily unless the pilot holds manual up-elevator.
How does it work? By propping the aircraft nose up by $+5^\circ \text{ to } +10^\circ$ (or $+3^\circ \text{ to } +5^\circ$ depending on wing incidence) when clicking Calibrate Level in Mission Planner, the autopilot registers that nose-up attitude as its artificial level reference datum. When flying straight in FBWA, the autopilot commands sufficient pitch to maintain continuous lift at cruise throttle.
Where is it used? All fixed-wing and flying-wing autonomous setups.
Example: Propping a small wooden block under the Nano Talon's nose wheel during Mission Planner level calibration to eliminate in-flight cruise altitude sink.
Prerequisites: Completed 6-axis accelerometer calibration.
4. In-Flight Background Auto-Trim (SERVO_AUTO_TRIM)
What is it? An ArduPlane background algorithm that monitors the integrator effort exerted by the PID controller to maintain level flight in FBWA and writes those offsets directly into the servo neutral parameters (SERVOx_TRIM).
Why do we use it? Eliminates the need to land, mechanically adjust pushrod clevises, or use transmitter trim switches to achieve straight-and-level flight. Prevents catastrophic pitch/roll deviations when transitioning from stabilized mode to manual mode.
How does it work? When parameter SERVO_AUTO_TRIM = 1 is set, the autopilot evaluates PID surface deflection during stable, uncommanded FBWA flight. Every 10 seconds, it updates the stored servo center positions in non-volatile memory. When the pilot switches to MANUAL, the control surfaces remain deflected at the aerodynamically trimmed position.
Where is it used? Initial fixed-wing commissioning sorties and post-airframe modification flights.
Example: After flying in FBWA for two minutes, landing and inspecting Mission Planner's Servo Output page reveals Output 2 trim has migrated from $1500\,\mu\text{s}$ to $1538\,\mu\text{s}$, compensating for a mechanical V-tail offset.
Prerequisites: Mechanically centered control surfaces and stable FBWA baseline tuning.

⚠️
The Servo Rail Power Rule During Bench Testing:
When connecting the aircraft back to Mission Planner to adjust SERVOx_MIN and SERVOx_MAX in the Servo Output tab, servos will not deflect unless the LiPo flight battery is plugged in. USB provides power exclusively to the internal MCU logic and receiver rail; the servo output bus relies entirely on the ESC's 5V BEC. Always remove the propeller from the motor shaft before connecting a battery on the workbench!

🛠️ Practical Implementation: Field Execution & Bench Correction

Painless360 outlines a disciplined, two-stage procedure spanning airfield launch execution and immediate post-flight workbench tuning:

Stage 1: Airfield Maiden Sortie Execution

  1. Spotter Integration: Never maiden alone. The pilot's eyes must remain locked on the aircraft; the spotter monitors airspace, verifies telemetry audio alerts, tracks the flight timer, and performs the physical hand-launch.
  2. Control Direction Sanity Check (The Two-Way Verification):
    • In MANUAL mode: Verify right roll raises right aileron/lowers left; pull elevator back raises both V-tail surfaces; push right rudder deflects surfaces for right yaw.
    • In FBWA mode: Physically lift right wing $\rightarrow$ right aileron must deflect down to oppose hand motion. Pitch nose down $\rightarrow$ both V-tails must deflect upward to raise the nose.
  3. Hand-Launch Sequence:
    • Select FBWA mode on the 3-position switch (Channel 8).
    • Advance throttle smoothly to $70\% - 80\%$.
    • Spotter executes a firm, level throw straight into the prevailing wind (never downwind or upward at a steep angle, which causes a tip stall).
    • Hands remain light on cyclic sticks; allow ArduPlane to level the wings and establish a climb attitude.
  4. Flight Envelope Exploration:
    • Climb to a safe testing altitude ($\ge 50\,\text{m}$ AGL).
    • Fly gentle figure-eights to evaluate bank angle clamping and roll response.
    • Release cyclic sticks at cruise throttle ($50\% - 60\%$) to observe whether the aircraft maintains altitude or gradually sinks.
    • Switch into CIRCLE mode to verify that the GPS receiver, compass heading, and navigational math successfully orbit the current point.
  5. Recovery & Landing: Line up final approach directly into the wind in FBWA. Manage descent rate using throttle; flare smoothly just above ground effect and belly-land on grass.

Stage 2: Workbench Diagnosis & Remediation Steps

  1. Correcting Altitude Bleed (Pitch Level Calibration):
    • Connect the Pixhawk to Mission Planner via direct USB.
    • Place a small wooden block or shim under the nose until the artificial horizon on the Flight Data HUD displays $+5^\circ \text{ to } +8^\circ$ nose-up pitch.
    • Navigate to Initial Setup -> Mandatory Hardware -> Accelerometer Calibration.
    • Click Calibrate Level.
    • Remove the shim. The HUD horizon will now sit slightly nose-down while resting flat on the desk, ensuring ArduPlane commands positive Angle of Attack during level cruise flight.
  2. Increasing Banking Agility (Parameter Adjustment):
    • Navigate to Config -> Full Parameter Tree.
    • Search for LIM_ROLL_CD.
    • Change the value from default 4500 ($45^\circ$) to 5500 ($55^\circ$) or 6000 ($60^\circ$).
    • Click Write Params to commit to non-volatile memory.
  3. Expanding Elevator Servo Throws:
    • Remove the propeller completely from the motor.
    • Connect the flight LiPo battery to energize the servo rail BEC.
    • Navigate to Initial Setup -> Mandatory Hardware -> Servo Output.
    • Locate Output 2 (V-tail Left) and Output 3 (V-tail Right).
    • Expand the Min down from $1100\,\mu\text{s}$ to $1000\,\mu\text{s}$ and the Max up from $1900\,\mu\text{s}$ to $2000\,\mu\text{s}$.
    • Deflect the radio elevator stick fully up/down; verify physical throws have increased without mechanical pushrod binding or servo buzzing.
  4. Auditing Background Learned Trims:
    • In the same Servo Output window, inspect the Trim column for Outputs 1, 2, and 3.
    • Verify that values have migrated from $1500\,\mu\text{s}$ to learned offsets (e.g., $1538\,\mu\text{s}$, $1472\,\mu\text{s}$).
    • Confirm that manual transmitter digital trim tabs remain dead-center ($0$).

⚠️ Common Problems & Solutions (Maiden Flight Troubleshooting)

Identified Problem Underlying Root Cause How to Identify Problem Engineering Remediation Why the Solution Works
Vehicle Sinks / Loses Altitude at Cruise Artificial horizon was calibrated with the fuselage sitting dead-level on a flat desk ($0^\circ$ pitch), resulting in insufficient wing Angle of Attack (AoA). In FBWA cruise at $60\%$ throttle, the aircraft gradually sinks unless continuous up-elevator stick pressure is applied. Prop up the aircraft nose by $+5^\circ \text{ to } +10^\circ$ on the bench and execute Calibrate Level in Mission Planner. Establishes a nose-up attitude datum as "zero level", forcing the autopilot to maintain positive wing lift ($L \propto C_L$) during straight-and-level flight.
Weak Elevator Authority / Inability to Climb Software servo travel endpoints (SERVOx_MIN/MAX) in ArduPlane constrain mechanical pushrod throw below aerodynamic requirements. Full backward stick deflection produces minimal upward control surface movement; aircraft climbs sluggishly even at full throttle. Energize the servo rail via LiPo battery (props off) and expand output endpoints (e.g., from $1100/1900\,\mu\text{s}$ to $1000/2000\,\mu\text{s}$). Expands the microsecond pulse window transmitted to the servo, allowing full mechanical horn throw and increased aerodynamic deflection.
Excessively Large Turning Circle in FBWA Maximum roll angle parameter LIM_ROLL_CD is clamped too low ($45^\circ$) for a fast, short-wingspan airframe. Holding full roll stick deflection produces a wide, sweeping turn that requires excessive airspace to reverse direction. Navigate to Full Parameter Tree and raise LIM_ROLL_CD from 4500 to 5500 or 6000. Allows the autopilot's cascaded attitude controller to bank the aircraft more steeply ($55^\circ - 60^\circ$), tightening the horizontal turn radius ($R = \frac{v^2}{g \tan\phi}$).
Pre-Arm Failure: PreArm: Check Airspeed Pitot tube airspeed sensor parameter is enabled in firmware, but no physical differential pressure sensor is connected. Red annunciator text on Mission Planner HUD; buzzer emits low rejection chime upon attempting to arm. In Full Parameter Tree, set ARSPD_TYPE = 0 and ARSPD_ENABLE = 0; click Write Params. Instructs the EKF to rely on GPS ground speed and synthetic aerodynamic calculations rather than reading an open, floating analog/I2C sensor port.
Erratic Circling in CIRCLE / LOITER ("Toilet-Bowling") Magnetometer experiencing electromagnetic interference (EMI) from battery leads or uncalibrated indoor magnetic hard-iron offsets. Aircraft enters expanding, accelerating spiral orbits instead of holding a clean, centered GPS circular track. Perform an outdoor Live Compass Calibration at the airfield away from reinforced concrete, cars, and metallic fences. Re-triangulates clean magnetic North offsets without distortion from indoor structural rebar or workshop high-current power supplies.

If the Angle of Attack offset is neglected, the PID controller must constantly saturate its integrator loop to hold up-elevator. If the pilot then switches into MANUAL mode at high speed, the aircraft will pitch violently upward into an aerodynamic loop or high-speed stall. Every adjustment must balance both manual and stabilized flight regimes.

📚 Technical Terminology

Term Technical Definition Why It Matters Practical Flight Context
FBWA (Fly-By-Wire A) An augmented stabilization flight mode where cyclic stick inputs command target bank and pitch angles directly. Prevents pilot-induced aerodynamic stalls and spin divergence during launches and initial sorties. The default launch mode recommended by ArduPlane engineers to neutralize motor torque-roll.
Angle of Attack (AoA) The acute angle between the chord line of the wing airfoil and the oncoming relative wind vector. Directly determines lift generation ($L \propto C_L$); insufficient AoA causes gradual altitude loss in level cruise. Compensated on the bench by propping the aircraft nose up $+5^\circ$ to $+10^\circ$ during level calibration.
LIM_ROLL_CD ArduPlane parameter specifying the maximum allowable bank angle in stabilized modes, measured in centidegrees ($1^\circ = 100\,\text{CD}$). Clamps how steeply the autopilot will bank the aircraft during full stick deflection. Raised from $4500$ ($45^\circ$) to $5500$ ($55^\circ$) to allow tight turning on the sub-1m Nano Talon.
SERVO_AUTO_TRIM An automated software routine that samples steady-state PID integrator effort and updates physical servo trims (SERVOx_TRIM). Eliminates manual transmitter sub-trim adjustments and ensures smooth mode transitions to MANUAL. Activated by setting parameter to 1; writes updated offsets to non-volatile EEPROM every 10 seconds.
Centidegrees A unit of angular measurement equal to one-hundredth of an angular degree ($0.01^\circ$). Used across ArduPilot's parameter tree to store high-precision angular limits as integers rather than floating-point values. A bank limit of $60^\circ$ is written into Mission Planner as $60 \times 100 = 6000\,\text{CD}$.
AutoTune A flight mode that executes real-time system identification algorithms to tune roll, pitch, and yaw PID gains automatically. Replaces tedious manual trial-and-error PID tuning with mathematical response modeling. Executed by rocking sticks rapidly left/right and up/down $\ge 20$ times in smooth air.
Toilet-Bowling An oscillatory flight divergence where an autonomous aircraft circles in expanding, accelerating spirals during GPS holding. Indicates compromised heading data, risking loss of control or flyaway. Caused by uncalibrated compass offsets or electromagnetic interference from high-current motor leads.

💡 Practical Takeaways: Field Operations & Workshop Rules

  • 🟢 Never Maiden Alone: Always fly with an experienced spotter. The pilot focuses exclusively on airframe dynamics; the spotter tracks flight time, monitors telemetry audio alerts, and checks airspace.
  • 🟢 Launch Exclusively in FBWA: Hand-launch the aircraft in Fly-By-Wire A mode at $70\% - 80\%$ throttle. The autopilot will automatically suppress motor torque-roll and establish a stable climb.
  • 🔴 Maintain MANUAL as the Escape Hatch: Always assign pure MANUAL to Switch Position 1. If PID loop oscillations or sensor faults threaten the aircraft, flipping to manual bypasses all autopilot intervention immediately.
  • 🟡 Always Prop the Nose Up for Level Calibration: When executing Calibrate Level in Mission Planner, shim the nose up $+5^\circ \text{ to } +10^\circ$ relative to the flat desk to prevent altitude bleed during cruise flight.
  • ⚠️ Remove Props When Tuning Servo Outputs: Modifying SERVOx_MIN/MAX endpoints requires LiPo battery power on the servo rail. Always remove propellers from the motor shaft before workbench testing.
  • 🟢 Capture As-Built Photographic Logs: Once the model is mechanically trimmed and validated, photograph all internal carrier board pinouts, receiver antenna routing, and ESC wire sequences for future reference.

📝 Quick Revision (High-Yield Facts)

  • The Maiden Trinity Switch: Position 1 = MANUAL (Override); Position 2 = FBWA (Stabilized Launch/Flight); Position 3 = CIRCLE (GPS Hold).
  • Correcting Altitude Sinks: Prop nose up $+5^\circ \text{ to } +10^\circ$ on the bench $\rightarrow$ click Calibrate Level in Mission Planner.
  • Tightening Turning Radius: Increase parameter LIM_ROLL_CD from 4500 ($45^\circ$) to 5500 ($55^\circ$) or 6000 ($60^\circ$).
  • Increasing Elevator Throw: Expand SERVOx_MIN and SERVOx_MAX on Outputs 2 & 3 by $\approx 100\,\mu\text{s}$ with LiPo connected (props removed).
  • Background Auto-Trim: Setting SERVO_AUTO_TRIM = 1 migrates flight PID integrator effort into SERVOx_TRIM every 10 seconds during level FBWA flight.
  • Airspeed Pre-Arm Bypass: If no pitot tube is installed, set ARSPD_TYPE = 0 and ARSPD_ENABLE = 0 to eliminate pre-arm lockout.

❓ Questions & Answers (Self-Assessment)

Q1: Why should an autonomous fixed-wing model be hand-launched in FBWA mode rather than MANUAL mode?
Single-motor pusher configurations generate substantial motor torque reaction ($\tau = P / \omega$) at launch throttle, which tends to roll the aircraft sharply along its longitudinal axis. Because hand-launches begin at near-stall airspeeds with minimal control surface authority, human pilot reaction time is often too slow to prevent a tip stall. In FBWA, the autopilot senses roll acceleration within milliseconds and applies corrective aileron automatically to hold wings level.
Q2: What causes an aircraft to slowly lose altitude during FBWA cruise flight, and how is it resolved?
It is caused by calibrating the accelerometer level datum with the fuselage resting perfectly horizontal on a flat bench. At $0^\circ$ pitch attitude, fixed-wing airfoils produce insufficient lift at cruise airspeed. The issue is resolved by propping the nose up by $+5^\circ \text{ to } +10^\circ$ using a shim and clicking Calibrate Level in Mission Planner, teaching the flight controller to hold a positive Angle of Attack (AoA) during straight-and-level flight.
Q3: Why must the LiPo battery be connected to test expanded servo throws on the bench, and what critical safety precaution is mandatory?
The Pixhawk's MAIN and AUX servo output rails are electrically isolated from the USB $+5\text{V}$ power bus to protect the internal MCU from inductive servo back-EMF spikes. The servo rail receives power exclusively from the ESC's 5V BEC, which requires the main flight battery to operate. The mandatory safety precaution is to remove the propeller completely from the motor shaft before connecting the battery on the bench.
Q4: What is the mechanical consequence of enabling SERVO_AUTO_TRIM = 1 during flight?
During straight-and-level flight in FBWA mode, the autopilot monitors the PID controller's integrator term (the steady-state deflection needed to maintain wings level). Every 10 seconds, it writes those offsets directly into the SERVOx_TRIM parameters in EEPROM. When the pilot subsequently switches into MANUAL mode, the surfaces remain deflected at the learned trim positions, ensuring the aircraft tracks straight without manual transmitter trim tab input.
Q5: How does increasing LIM_ROLL_CD from 4500 to 5500 alter the aircraft's turning characteristics?
Parameter LIM_ROLL_CD represents the maximum bank angle limit in centidegrees. Increasing it from $4500$ ($45^\circ$) to $5500$ ($55^\circ$) allows the autopilot to bank the airframe $10^\circ$ more steeply during full lateral stick deflection. By the physics of coordinated horizontal turns ($R = \frac{v^2}{g \tan\phi}$), a higher bank angle $\phi$ significantly reduces the turning radius $R$, providing tighter maneuvering for compact airframes.
